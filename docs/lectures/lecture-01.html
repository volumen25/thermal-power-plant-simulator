Lecture 1: Boiler Efficiency

Published

January 4, 2027

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Define boiler thermal efficiency and distinguish it from combustion efficiency
  • Apply the direct method to calculate boiler efficiency from plant data
  • Calculate LHV from HFO elemental composition using the Dulong formula
  • Identify the key factors that influence boiler efficiency in practice
  • Interpret efficiency differences across fuel types and operating loads

1. What Is Efficiency?

Efficiency is the ratio of useful output to energy input. It quantifies how well a system converts an input resource into a desired output, without waste.

\[\eta = \frac{\text{Useful output}}{\text{Energy input}}\]

Note

Efficiency and effectiveness are often used interchangeably in everyday language, but they mean different things in engineering:

  • Efficiency — minimising waste; doing things right
  • Effectiveness — maximising output; doing the right things

A boiler can be highly effective (producing large amounts of steam) while being inefficient (wasting significant amounts of fuel energy).

2. Boiler Efficiency — Two Definitions

Combustion efficiency measures the burner’s ability to completely burn fuel. It accounts for unburnt fuel and excess air in the exhaust. A burner with high combustion efficiency releases nearly all the chemical energy available in the fuel.

Thermal efficiency measures the heat exchanger’s ability to transfer heat from the combustion gases to the water and steam. Even with perfect combustion, heat can be lost through the stack, radiation from boiler surfaces, and blowdown.

In practice, boiler thermal efficiency is the combined result of both processes and is the primary performance indicator used in power plant operations:

\[\eta_{\text{boiler}} = \frac{\text{Energy transferred to steam}}{\text{Energy released by fuel}}\]

3. Boiler Thermal Efficiency — Direct Method

The direct (input/output) method computes efficiency from measured steam and fuel quantities. Let:

  • \(\dot{m}_s\) = steam mass flow rate (kg/s)
  • \(h_2\) = specific enthalpy of steam at boiler outlet (kJ/kg)
  • \(h_1\) = specific enthalpy of feedwater at boiler inlet (kJ/kg)
  • \(\dot{m}_f\) = fuel mass flow rate (kg/s)
  • \(HV\) = heating value of fuel (kJ/kg)

Since steam is generated at constant pressure, the heat absorbed per kg of steam is \((h_2 - h_1)\). The boiler thermal efficiency is therefore:

\[\eta_{\text{boiler}} = \frac{\dot{m}_s (h_2 - h_1)}{\dot{m}_f \times HV} \times 100\%\]

Measurement Boundary

The boundary defines what is included in the calculation. For a complete boiler system:

Feedwater inlet (h₁) → [Economizer → Steam drum → Superheater] → Steam outlet (h₂)
                                         ↑
                               Fuel input (ṁ_f × HV)

The economizer is inside the boundary — its heat recovery is credited to the boiler. The feedwater enthalpy \(h_1\) is measured at the economizer water inlet, not at the feedpump outlet.

4. Fuel Heating Values

Higher and Lower Heating Value

Fuel contains hydrogen which, on combustion, produces water vapour. The Higher Heating Value (HHV) assumes this water condenses and its latent heat is recovered. The Lower Heating Value (LHV) assumes water remains as vapour and the latent heat is lost with the flue gas.

Boiler efficiency in marine and power engineering is always quoted on the LHV basis because the flue gas exits above the dew point and the latent heat is not recovered in practice.

Dulong Formula

LHV can be calculated from elemental composition:

\[LHV = HHV - 2442 \times (9H + W)\]

Where:

  • \(H\) = hydrogen mass fraction
  • \(W\) = moisture mass fraction
  • \(2442\) kJ/kg = latent heat of water vapour at 25°C
  • \(9H\) = mass of water formed per kg fuel from hydrogen combustion
# HFO elemental composition (mass fractions) — X008xx tags
C = 84.0 / 100   # X00800 carbon
H = 13.4 / 100   # X00801 hydrogen
S = 0.6  / 100   # X00802 sulfur
O_frac = 1.5 / 100  # X00803 oxygen
N = 0.5  / 100   # X00804 nitrogen
W = 1.0  / 100   # X00807 moisture

HHV = 41303.16   # kJ/kg  H00810

LHV = HHV - 2442 * (9 * H + W)

print(f"HHV              : {HHV:.2f} kJ/kg")
print(f"LHV (Dulong)     : {LHV:.2f} kJ/kg")
print(f"HHV - LHV        : {HHV - LHV:.2f} kJ/kg")
print(f"Hydrogen content : {H*100:.1f}% — drives the HHV/LHV gap")
HHV              : 41303.16 kJ/kg
LHV (Dulong)     : 38333.69 kJ/kg
HHV - LHV        : 2969.47 kJ/kg
Hydrogen content : 13.4% — drives the HHV/LHV gap

5. Steam Properties and Enthalpy

Accurate enthalpy values are essential for boiler efficiency calculations. We use the IAPWS-IF97 standard via the iapws library.

from iapws import IAPWS97

# Boiler operating conditions from simulator
P_steam = 158.48 * 0.1       # bara → MPa
T_steam = 535.85 + 273.15    # °C → K
P_fw    = 201.97 * 0.1       # bara → MPa
T_fw    = 254.47 + 273.15    # °C → K

steam    = IAPWS97(T=T_steam, P=P_steam)
feedwater = IAPWS97(T=T_fw,   P=P_fw)

print(f"HP steam:")
print(f"  Temperature : {steam.T - 273.15:.2f} °C")
print(f"  Pressure    : {steam.P:.2f} MPa")
print(f"  Enthalpy    : {steam.h:.2f} kJ/kg")
print(f"\nFeedwater:")
print(f"  Temperature : {feedwater.T - 273.15:.2f} °C")
print(f"  Pressure    : {feedwater.P:.2f} MPa")
print(f"  Enthalpy    : {feedwater.h:.2f} kJ/kg")
print(f"\nEnthalpy rise : {steam.h - feedwater.h:.2f} kJ/kg")
HP steam:
  Temperature : 535.85 °C
  Pressure    : 15.85 MPa
  Enthalpy    : 3402.25 kJ/kg

Feedwater:
  Temperature : 254.47 °C
  Pressure    : 20.20 MPa
  Enthalpy    : 1107.61 kJ/kg

6. Worked Example — HFO at 230 MW

Using simulator data from the 230 MW HFO operating condition:

# Simulator tag values
h_steam = 3400.8    # kJ/kg  H02603  HP line steam enthalpy
h_fw    = 1097.1    # kJ/kg  H02448  economizer water inlet enthalpy
G_steam = 716.17 / 3.6   # kg/s  G02600  HP line steam flow
G_fuel  = 52.42  / 3.6   # kg/s  G01038  HFO supply flow to boiler

# Heat flows
Q_steam = G_steam * (h_steam - h_fw)
Q_fuel  = G_fuel  * LHV
eta     = Q_steam / Q_fuel

print(f"Steam flow       : {G_steam:.4f} kg/s")
print(f"Fuel flow        : {G_fuel:.4f} kg/s")
print(f"Enthalpy rise    : {h_steam - h_fw:.2f} kJ/kg")
print(f"Heat to steam    : {Q_steam:.1f} kW")
print(f"Fuel heat input  : {Q_fuel:.1f} kW")
print(f"Boiler efficiency: {eta:.1%}")
Steam flow       : 198.9361 kg/s
Fuel flow        : 14.5611 kg/s
Enthalpy rise    : 2303.70 kJ/kg
Heat to steam    : 458289.1 kW
Fuel heat input  : 558181.1 kW
Boiler efficiency: 82.1%

7. Factors Affecting Boiler Efficiency

Fuel Type

Different fuels have different hydrogen content, moisture, and ash levels, all of which affect the HHV/LHV gap and combustion behaviour.

Fuel Typical LHV (kJ/kg) H content Notes
HFO 38,000–40,500 ~13% High H → large HHV/LHV gap
Coal 24,000–30,000 ~5% High ash, soot fouling
Biofuel (pellets) 17,000–19,000 ~6% High moisture
Natural gas 47,000–50,000 ~25% Cleanest combustion

Boiler Load

Boiler efficiency is not constant across the load range. At part load:

  • Fixed losses (radiation, convection) become a larger fraction of fuel input
  • Excess air tends to increase, raising stack losses
  • Heat transfer surfaces may be oversized, altering flue gas temperatures

Soot Fouling

Soot deposits on heat transfer surfaces act as insulation, reducing heat transfer from flue gas to steam. This raises flue gas outlet temperature and stack losses, directly reducing efficiency.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Illustrative efficiency vs load curve
load = np.array([30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100])
eta_clean = np.array([78, 80, 81, 82, 82.5, 82.1, 81.5, 80.5])
eta_soot  = np.array([74, 76, 77, 78, 78.5, 78.0, 77.5, 76.5])

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))
ax.plot(load, eta_clean, "bo-", linewidth=2, markersize=6, label="Clean surfaces")
ax.plot(load, eta_soot,  "ro--", linewidth=2, markersize=6, label="Soot fouling")
ax.fill_between(load, eta_soot, eta_clean, alpha=0.15, color="gray",
                label="Efficiency penalty")
ax.set_title("Boiler Efficiency vs Load — Effect of Soot Fouling")
ax.set_xlabel("Boiler Load (%)")
ax.set_ylabel("Thermal Efficiency (%)")
ax.grid(True, alpha=0.3)
ax.legend()
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

9. Summary

  • Boiler thermal efficiency = energy to steam / energy from fuel
  • Always use LHV as the fuel energy basis
  • LHV is calculated from HHV using the Dulong formula
  • Accurate enthalpy values require steam tables (IAPWS-IF97)
  • Efficiency is influenced by fuel type, load, and surface cleanliness
  • In Lab 1 you will calculate efficiency for four conditions: HFO at 230 MW, biofuel at 80%, coal at 80%, and coal with soot
